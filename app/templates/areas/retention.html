Left after year one is computed from head counts rather than the published retention rate, which IPEDS rounds to two decimals — enough to report two schools four hundred students apart as identical. It describes students who started one year before the year shown, so it is a far more recent cohort than the graduation figures, which follow students who started eight years earlier. Both are facts about the school; neither is a stage in the other’s story. Took longer is the six-year rate minus the four-year rate — the share of a cohort that finishes, but not on time, and pays for the extra year. Spread by race is the distance between the best and worst reported group on the six-year rate. We compute both; nobody publishes either. The four-year rate is itself derived: IPEDS ships a completion_rate_4yr that reads zero for every institution here, so it is computed from the underlying award counts and checked against the published six-year rate, which it reproduces exactly. Groups with fewer than {{ 30 }} students in the cohort are left out, and two categories are excluded from the race spread entirely — international students and students whose ethnicity went unrecorded describe a registrar's filing rather than a background a student would claim.
