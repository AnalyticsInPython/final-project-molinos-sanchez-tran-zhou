{# The long form of the line above the charts, which is one sentence now (see retention.year_meaning): a card carrying two notices cannot also carry a three-line note about cohorts before its first chart. #} The year on this card is when IPEDS reported these figures, not when anyone enrolled. Every graduation figure under it follows one class, with one cohort count, whose awards are tallied at four years and again at six — so the four-year rate is not a later class than the six-year rate beside it, and neither is a class that graduated in the year on the card. Left after year one is computed from head counts rather than the published retention rate, which IPEDS rounds to two decimals — enough to report two schools four hundred students apart as identical. It describes students who started one year before the year shown, so it is a far more recent cohort than the graduation figures, which follow students who started seven years earlier. Both are facts about the school; neither is a stage in the other’s story. Took longer is the six-year rate minus the four-year rate — the share of a cohort that finishes, but not on time, and pays for the extra year. IPEDS publishes neither figure. The four-year rate is itself derived: IPEDS ships a completion_rate_4yr that reads zero for every institution here, so it is computed from the underlying award counts and checked against the published six-year rate, which it reproduces exactly. A school whose cohort is under 30 students is shown as unreported rather than as a rate one person could move by several points.
